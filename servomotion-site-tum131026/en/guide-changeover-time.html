Where Does Changeover Time Actually Go?

Changeover time is not one operation but a stack of small steps. Here we separate those steps, identify which one actually consumes the time and show where a visible reference helps.

Efficiency opportunity

The gain grows with how often you repeat it, not with the minutes saved on a single changeover

  • Record changeover time, repeated setups and start-up scrap for one week.
  • Leave out the mechanical work a visible reference does not affect.
  • Compare the result in annual downtime and operator hours.

LET'S CALCULATE THE GAIN FOR YOUR LINE

What “setup takes half an hour” actually contains

Ask a line supervisor how long a product change takes and you usually get a single figure. That figure is the sum of five very different jobs:

  1. Transferring the measurement. Turning the value in the recipe into a physical position on the machine, using a tape measure, a template or the operator’s memory.
  2. Finding the setup point. Marking where the blade, guide or stop has to go. Usually the most repeated step of all.
  3. The physical adjustment. Loosening, moving and re-tightening the element. This step is mechanical and is not shortened by an alignment system.
  4. Verification. The “is it right?” check. Without a reference this step means measuring — and measuring means time.
  5. Checking the first metres. Looking at the result once material has run through, and repeating the setup if needed.

Three of those five steps — transferring the measurement, finding the setup point and verification — are an information problem, not a mechanical one. A visible reference affects those three directly.

Where to measure

Instead of timing the changeover as a whole, put a stopwatch on each of the five steps separately. On most lines the surprise shows up on the first attempt — and it is usually not where you expected.

Rework: the invisible line item

When the setup does not hold first time, the steps run again from the top. The second round repeats almost all of the first; by the third the operator’s morale is part of the cost.

The number of repeats is rarely recorded. Yet if one product change in three goes to a second round, the real setup time is significantly above what the records show.

What does a visible reference change?

Projecting the laser reference lines onto the working surface shortens three of the steps:

StepWithout a referenceWith a visible reference
Transferring the measurementTape or template, dependent on the operatorThe recipe is entered at the panel and the line moves to position
Finding the setup pointMarking and searchingThe point is already visible
VerificationMeasuring againVisual comparison
Physical adjustmentUnchanged — this step is mechanical

Why the effect grows on lines that change often

The gain in setup time is multiplied by the changeover frequency. On a line that changes product once a week, five minutes goes unnoticed. On a line that changes three times a shift, those same five minutes add up to several hours a month.

That is why the first question to ask is not “how long does setup take” but “how many times a day do we set up” .

For your own line

Keep three numbers for one week: product changes per day · time spent on each change · the number of times the setup did not hold first time. Those three numbers are enough to show whether an alignment investment pays for you.
