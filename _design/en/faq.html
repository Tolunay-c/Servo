FAQ

QUESTIONS
BEFORE THE DECISION

The questions that come up most often in the first conversation — accuracy, system selection, integration, installation, commercial terms, safety.

ACCURACY

What does ±1.00 mm mean?

It is the deviation limit of the projected line against its target position. It is not the cutting tolerance of your product but the accuracy of the position the line shows. Cut quality comes from the blade, tension, material and operator setting together.

Does that value stay the same at every distance?

No. As the working distance grows, small deviations in mounting angle turn into larger shifts on the surface. Distance and mounting rigidity are therefore measured during the site survey, and accuracy is assessed together with those conditions.

Does the system lose its setting over time?

Position is held in closed loop by the servo axis; a power cut or a recipe change does not disturb it. The mounting structure has to be rigid so the mechanical reference is not lost — one of the items checked during the survey.

SYSTEM SELECTION

Core or Shuttle — how do we decide?

The deciding question is how many cutting lines have to be visible at the same time. Up to four lines Core is usually enough; five to six is borderline; above seven the shuttle architecture takes over.

What happens if the number of lines grows later?

In the shuttle architecture the body is dimensioned for the densest foreseeable recipe and a reserve zone is left. Carriages can be added later without changing the body. On the Core side capacity is limited by the body dimension.

How do we determine the stroke range of our line?

The real travel range between the narrowest and the widest recipe is the stroke; it is not the same as the total working width of the machine. Body and rail length follow from this value.

INTEGRATION AND OPERATION

Will it connect to our existing automation?

The system runs on an industrial PLC and servo drive; data exchange with line automation is over a standard fieldbus. Which protocol and which data structure will be used is defined together before commissioning.

Who defines the recipes?

Recipes can be defined from the operator panel and, optionally, transferred from line automation. During commissioning your existing recipes are entered and verified together.

What is the panel like to use?

The operator panel is a touch panel; recipe selection, position display and status monitoring all happen on the same screen. Use is handed over during commissioning, working through it with the operators and the maintenance team.

INSTALLATION

Who does the installation?

Installation and commissioning are within our scope. The installation window is set together according to your production plan; details are on the service page.

How long do we have to stop the line?

The duration depends on body length, mounting structure and access conditions on the line. A realistic installation window can be given once those three are measured during the survey.

Is a new supporting structure needed?

If the existing frame is rigid enough the system is mounted on it; if not, a new cross-beam is designed. That decision is made during the survey, together with vibration and access conditions.

COMMERCIAL

What information do you need for a quotation?

Three pieces of information are usually enough for a first assessment: line type, working stroke and the number of lines that must be visible at the same time. The application summary tool on the contact page lets you send these in one go.

Is the site survey chargeable?

The survey is part of the quotation process; its scope and conditions are clarified in the first conversation.

What is the delivery time?

It depends on body dimension, laser count and the production schedule at the time. It is stated in writing, project by project, in the quotation.

Warranty and spare parts?

Warranty period, scope and response conditions vary by project and are defined in writing in the quotation. The critical spare parts list is agreed together at handover.

SAFETY AND MAINTENANCE

Are the lasers safe for the operator?

Industrial line laser modules are used. The laser class of the module is stated on the product label and in the user manual delivered with the system; reflection and viewing angle are also assessed together during installation. In all cases the beam must not be viewed directly and the modules must be operated according to the conditions in the manual.

What needs attention during maintenance?

Periodic maintenance comes down to the single servo axis and the cleanliness of the laser optics. The system must be de-energised and the line brought to a safe state during maintenance.
